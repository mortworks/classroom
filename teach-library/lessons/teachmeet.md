## Private Eye

!layout: image

{{img:mate-this-is-epic}}

Humour clarifies abstraction.

:::
Cartoons from Private Eye that have made me laugh.

I keep these because humour often crystallises an idea more sharply than exposition. A single image can unlock a discussion, shift tone, or reframe a concept in seconds.
:::

## The Phoenix

!layout: image

{{img:homework-cow}}

Comics can teach structure.

:::
Comic strips from my son's copies of The Phoenix.

Narrative sequencing, visual compression, conceptual clarity — these strips are often better structured than many worksheets.
:::

## Claude

!layout: image

{{img:roman-campagna}}

The classical pastoral.

:::
Pastoral landscape paintings by Claude.

When teaching pastoral poetry, I want pupils to see what the aesthetic actually looked like — not just hear the term.
:::

## Samuel Palmer

!layout: image

{{img:magic-apple-tree}}

What Blake changed.

:::
A different pastoral painting by Samuel Palmer — as a means of illustrating what William Blake's poetry did for the pastoral that was new.

The shift becomes visible when placed side by side with Claude.
:::
